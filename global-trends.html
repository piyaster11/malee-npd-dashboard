What it is and who sells it Real public data

The water held inside the banana, released with enzymes and separated in a centrifuge. It is sold as a single-ingredient, “coconut water 2.0” hydration drink.[2][1]

Woodstock Organic Banana Water
UNFI private label, shown at Expo West, Mar 2024, $2.99 MSRP. Made by FruitaBio in Thailand from two heirloom Thai banana varieties; pulp and peel go into PHA bioplastic. 16.9 oz can: 80 kcal, 19 g sugar (none added), 450 mg potassium; “Product of Thailand”.[2][3]
Banagua
Nashville, launched Jul 2025. Organic, extracted in Thailand from two Thai heirloom varieties. Four SKUs (Original, Strawberry, Cocoa Vanilla, Blueberry), SRP $3 per 11.2 oz can. 3,000+ doors within four months: Sprouts, Kroger, Albertsons divisions, Erewhon, Fresh Thyme. Raised $5.5M from Hingham Growth Partners (2026) and expects a little over $5M revenue in year one.[6][5][4]
Banam
Ireland, Oct 2026: Tesco, BWG, Dunnes Stores. 250 ml can, “water of two bananas”, 37 kcal, three flavours. Name = banana + Thai “naam”.[7]
Note on Banana Wave
Banana Wave (Florida, 2016) is a banana-oat milk, not a banana water: 32 oz aseptic carton, $3.99–$5.99, Whole Foods, Walmart, H-E-B. It is in a different category.[8][9]

Search interest and how big it is

Real public data Google Trends US: “banana water” averaged 9.1 over the last 52 weeks vs 5.0 the year before (+82% Estimate). It peaked in the week of May 17, 2026 and has eased since. That is about 17% of coconut water’s search level (52.4) Estimate. The term also picks up “banana peel water for plants” searches, so the real drink interest is likely lower.[50]

Real public data The nearest category figure: SPINS shelf-stable coconut + plant water in the US, $751M, +20.5% (52 w/e 12 Jul 2026).[10]

Not found No public market size or growth rate for banana water itself; no Mintel, Innova or SPINS banana-water figure found in public press.

Fit with Malee — honest score Judgement sample

3.5 / 5 — worth a small test, not a big bet

For: the category’s whole story is Thai bananas, and the leading brands already make it in Thailand. It sits next to coconut water on shelf, and Malee already sells coconut water and runs canning and aseptic lines (public product range). It fits export buyers in the US, UK/Ireland and the Gulf, where low-sugar drinks are taxed less (about 3.8 g sugar per 100 ml for Woodstock’s can Estimate).

Against: the enzyme extraction and centrifuge step and the use of the biomass are specialised, and an established Thai maker (FruitaBio) already supplies one brand. It could be a partner or a rival. The category is small and young (~$5M year-one revenue for the leader), US search interest is easing after a spring peak, and the drink tastes sweet (19 g sugar per can).
